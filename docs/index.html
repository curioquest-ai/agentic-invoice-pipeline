The goal · row 2, expanded

"Structured output" means three different things here

Row 2 is the one people assume is equivalent across the three. It is not — and the ranking is the opposite of what price suggests.
TrackMechanismWhat it actually guarantees
A localformat=<json schema>Strongest. Constrained decoding — the generated tokens cannot leave your schema
B APItool_choice={"type":"tool"}A tool_use block — not that its contents validate. strict:true would, but needs additionalProperties:false, which Pydantic does not emit
C frameworkstructured_predictWeakest. Routes through tool-calling, so you lose the decoding guarantee A gave you for free
Key learningThe free, local track has the strongest structured-output guarantee of the three. Price does not buy correctness here — it buys a different set of trade-offs, which is exactly what you are here to measure.
GotchaNone of the three removes your validation layer. Structured output removes a class of parsing bugs; Invoice.model_validate() and validators.py are still what catch a wrong answer.
Framing

Demo AI fails in enterprises because nobody can measure it

  • A demo needs one document to work once. A pipeline needs 10,000 documents to work measurably — including the mistyped, the truncated, and the mathematically wrong ones.
  • The gap between the two isn't a better model. It's ground truth, an eval, and validators — everything we build before and after the model call.
  • Every module today ends with a scoreboard artifact, and they chain: form → dataset → parser → eval.
Key learningThe pipeline around the model is the product. The model is a swappable part.

M1 · Theory

The real trade-off is a triangle: cost, control, time-to-ship

  • "Open source is free" ignores GPU + maintenance cost. "Paid is expensive" ignores that ₹0.40/doc beats a week of engineer time.
  • The choice repeats at every layer, and the answer differs per layer. Mixed stacks are the norm, not a compromise.
  • We'll do the per-document arithmetic at 15:45 — with real numbers from this room.

Rule of the day

Generate ground truth before you generate documents

Because our bot fills the form, the true value of every field exists before the document does. We are not labeling documents — we are manufacturing documents from labels.

That one ordering decision is what makes the 15:00 eval possible. Teams that reverse it end up hand-labeling PDFs at midnight — or shipping extraction pipelines whose accuracy they cannot state.

M3 · Parser

Extraction transcribes; validation judges — never let the model do both

LLM (temperature 0):  "copy what is printed, verbatim, even the mistakes"
        │
        ▼  Invoice (Pydantic)
validators.py (code):  GSTIN mod-36 checksum · Σitems + CGST + SGST ≈ total
                       CGST == SGST · date parseable · per-line qty×rate ≈ amount
Key learningEvery check on the right is boring, fast, and testable — exactly why it must not be delegated to the model.
GotchaJSON in a text response ≠ structured output — use tool-calling / JSON-schema mode or you'll spend 20 minutes regex-ing markdown fences. And schema mode guarantees valid JSON, not terminating JSON: with an unbounded array a model can emit elements forever. One of our invoices ran 30,215 tokens over 33 minutes with no error, because a hang is not an exception. Cap num_predict; bound the array.
M3 · Parser · why B and C exist

Two patterns you cannot learn from the free track

Track B — what "structured output" means
tool_choice={"type": "tool",
             "name": "record_invoice"}
The model answers through your schema instead of writing prose you dig JSON out of. No fences, no coerce_json(), no regex. The pattern outlives the vendor — OpenAI's is response_format + strict.
Track C — the two-stage pattern
PDF ──► LlamaParse ──► markdown
                          │
                          ▼
              extract against Invoice
Stage 1 turns any document — scans, rotated tables, a 40-page contract — into clean markdown. Stage 2 is plain extraction. The framework buys you stage 1.
Key learningForced tool-choice guarantees a tool_use block — not that it validates. That needs strict:true, which needs additionalProperties:false, which Pydantic does not emit. Structured output removes a class of parsing bugs. It does not remove your validation layer.
Gotcha — we got this wrong, and measured itWe expected LlamaParse to be overkill on born-digital PDFs. Over 100 documents, same model in stage 2, Track C beat Track A 82.8% to 72.9% — winning every line-item field (rate +24, amount +15, HSN +15). Not text fidelity: structure. A markdown table with named columns beats a flat run of numbers. The price is 3× the latency. Now you can argue about it with numbers.

M4 · Eval

A validator that flags everything scores 100% recall

Line in your reportOur first runWhat it actually tells you
Validator recall on broken docs10/10 caughtlooks perfect — and means almost nothing alone
False flags on clean docs59 of 60it flagged nearly every clean document too
Validator precision16%the number that says whether a flag is worth acting on
Headline vs parsed-only66.6% → 72.1%a gap this size means a stability problem, not an accuracy one
Your leaderboard row is track | norm_acc | parse_fail | flag_prec | p50 | cost/doc — six columns, and two of them are on this slide.
Key learningRecall and precision together, or neither. A parser that transcribes badly flags everything, scores perfect recall, and is useless. Proof from our own runs: the same validators scored 16% precision behind Track A and 100% behind Track B. The validators never changed — only the parser did. A precision collapse is a parser problem wearing a validator costume.
GotchaA parse failure takes zero credit on every field, so a run that got less stable and a run that got less accurate look identical in the headline. That is what the parsed-only number separates.
M4 · Eval · inv_0085

Three flags fired on that one invoice. Two of them are real.

The numbers all three flags are arguing about
validation_flagVerdictWhy
CGST_SGST_UNEQUALTRUE positivethe printed document really is broken — that is the corruption we planted

Key learningYour validator cannot tell a broken document from a broken parser — both arrive as the same JSON. That third row is where 16% precision comes from, and why a flag count is not an error count.
GotchaIt gets worse on clean documents. Our parser dropped a plainly-printed GSTIN to null on 29 of the 60 clean invoices, manufacturing a GSTIN_MISSING alarm every time — and 52 of 60 clean documents ended up carrying at least one false flag. Fix the parser and the validator gets quieter, which is not obvious until you split true from false.

M5 · Synthesis

Production is mostly the broken 8%: retries, audit, human-in-loop

What today's pipeline doesn't have — and a production one must:
  • Queues + retries — one flaky call must not drop an invoice
  • Batch-level dedup — duplicate invoice_no is a cross-doc check; today's validators are per-doc (we planted dupes; did anyone catch them?)
  • Audit log — every extraction traceable: which doc, which model, which prompt version
  • Human-review routing — validation flags + low confidence → a review queue, not straight to the ERP
  • Drift watch — the vendor redesigns their invoice template; your eval harness is how you notice
  • Injection defense — document content is data, never instructions (rule 5 of the prompt; a hostile invoice is an attack surface)
Key learning~80% of production effort goes to the 8% broken docs. Budget accordingly.
